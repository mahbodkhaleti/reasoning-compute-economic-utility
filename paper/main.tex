\begin{abstract}
We study how test-time reasoning affects economic decision quality and how the measured returns translate into incentives for compute. Our evaluation covers four checkpoints and 216 auction-grounded decision scenarios from three eBay product categories. Candidate bids are constructed independently of the optimum, option order is randomized, and decisions based on win probabilities from a construction split are scored on held-out auctions. Increasing deterministic reasoning from 0 to 768 tokens raises mean utility ratio by 0.111--0.175; paired 95\% intervals exclude zero for every checkpoint, although the frontier is not locally monotone. Free-form and auction-CDF sensitivities preserve this endpoint result. We use five-seed stochastic frontiers in a calibrated contest between two modeled strategic agents. After normalizing payoffs by the private prize, allocations depend on relative compute cost $\kappa=\lambda/V$, relative social quality value $\rho=\beta/V$, and contest sensitivity $\gamma$. The resulting phase maps contain regions of overcomputation, alignment, and undercomputation: larger $\rho$ raises planner compute, while larger $\gamma$ strengthens private incentives to compute. These strategic results are conditional comparative statics, rather than observations of market behavior; the contribution is the held-out economic calibration and its uncertainty propagation through the game.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Test-time reasoning makes inference compute an input to decision quality. A deployed AI system could allocate additional compute after receiving a problem and improve its action without changing model parameters \citep{snell2024scaling,wu2024inference,alomrani2025budget}. For a centralized system, the allocation rule is familiar: purchase another unit of compute when its expected benefit exceeds its marginal cost. Competitive settings add a relative return. Better reasoning may improve a system's position in auctions, procurement, bargaining, pricing, or resource allocation.

Private and social returns can then diverge. In our modeled contest, two symmetric agents choose compute because quality raises their probability of capturing a prize, whereas a planner values winner quality net of both agents' costs. Decentralized compute can exceed, equal, or fall below the planner allocation, depending on compute cost, the social value of quality, and the strength of the competitive return. The object of interest is compute misallocation, $C_{\mathrm{NE}}\neq C_{\mathrm{OPT}}$, whose direction depends on these economic primitives.

The analysis has an empirical stage and a strategic stage. In the empirical task, an LLM receives an externally assigned reasoning budget, a private value, and a candidate set with historical win probabilities, then selects a bid. This auction-grounded design gives an exact expected-utility score. The resulting model-specific compute--quality frontier becomes an input to a reduced-form two-agent contest, where we compare symmetric Nash compute with a planner allocation.

The separation between these stages is important. The evaluated LLMs neither choose their token budgets nor compete with one another; exogenous budget assignments identify the frontier used in the game. The strategic analysis therefore describes calibrated comparative statics over stated parameter ranges. It does not estimate strategic behavior in deployed markets.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We make four contributions. First, we construct a leakage-free evaluation with controlled reasoning budgets, held-out scoring, and cardinal auction utility as the primary outcome. Second, we estimate discrete compute--quality frontiers for four checkpoints under a common constrained readout, with complementary free-form and auction-CDF sensitivity analyses. Third, we distinguish scenario-composition uncertainty from stochastic-generation uncertainty and propagate the latter through the game. Finally, we characterize overcompute, aligned, and undercompute regions in normalized $(\kappa,\rho,\gamma)$ space and examine their sensitivity to the parameterization and compute menu. The empirical calibration and uncertainty analysis distinguish this work from prior models of strategic test-time-compute competition.

\section{Related work}

Research on language-model reasoning has progressed from eliciting intermediate reasoning traces to treating inference computation as an allocation problem \citep{wei2022chain,snell2024scaling,wu2024inference}. Performance depends not only on model scale but also on the allocation of test-time resources. Recent surveys distinguish token budgets, repeated sampling, verifier-guided search, and adaptive stopping as separate inference regimes that require protocol-specific accounting \citep{alomrani2025budget,hariri2026scaling}. Work on uncertainty uses answer probabilities, self-reported confidence, and sequential or conformal rules to trade off compute, error, and coverage \citep{jurayj2025finalanswer,shi2025reasoninguncertainty,xie2026statistical}. We measure a token--utility frontier, separate scenario from generation uncertainty, and carry that uncertainty into an economic model; calibrated confidence is evaluated separately for stopping and abstention.

Economic evaluations of LLMs include tests of rational choice and microeconomic reasoning as well as agent benchmarks in unknown or strategically interactive environments \citep{raman2024steer,raman2025steerme,fish2025econevals}. Auction and bargaining studies examine planning, execution, behavior relative to economic benchmarks, and allocative efficiency \citep{chen2024aucarena,zhu2024synthetic,deng2024bargaining}. Our controlled decision task complements these benchmarks by using cardinal expected utility and regret to construct a compute--utility frontier for a subsequent strategic model. Sensitivity of multiple-choice evaluation to option order and readout protocol \citep{pezeshkpour2024order} motivates independent candidate generation, repeated order randomization, positional baselines, and a common constrained readout.

The strategic stage draws on models of costly competition. Classic rent-seeking theory studies effort that improves a contestant's probability of capturing a prize even when duplicated effort is socially wasteful \citep{tullock1980efficient}; \citet{artola2026games} apply this logic to test-time-compute competition among LLM service providers. We take this mechanism as given and focus on its auction-grounded empirical calibration. Model-specific reasoning-compute--utility frontiers, estimated with leakage controls and held-out scoring, determine the contest inputs; we carry their sampling and generation uncertainty into the equilibrium and planner benchmarks.

\section{Economic framework}

\subsection{Auction decision problem}

In the empirical task, a bidder model is supplied with private value $v$ and selects a bid $b\leq v$ from the candidate set. We approximate the price required to win by a random historical closing price $P$. The empirical winning probability and expected utility are
\begin{equation}
 \widehat p(b)=\widehat F_P(b)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}\{P_n\leq b\},
 \qquad U(b\mid v)=\widehat p(b)(v-b).
 \label{eq:auctionutility}
\end{equation}
This reduced-form calculation treats an observed closing price as the price a bidder must beat and imposes risk neutrality. It is not a structural estimate of rivals' values or a simulated equilibrium of the original eBay auctions. For scenario $s$, candidate set $B_s$, and private value $v_s$, the benchmark action is $b_s^*=\arg\max_{b\in B_s}U_s(b\mid v_s)$. Model $m$ at externally assigned reasoning budget $c$ selects $\widehat b_{msc}$. We report scenario-level exact optimality, regret, and utility ratio:
\begin{equation}
 A_{msc}=\mathbf{1}\{\widehat b_{msc}=b_s^*\},\quad
 R_{msc}=U_s(b_s^*\mid v_s)-U_s(\widehat b_{msc}\mid v_s),\quad
 Q_{msc}=\frac{U_s(\widehat b_{msc}\mid v_s)}{U_s(b_s^*\mid v_s)}.
 \label{eq:metrics}
\end{equation}
The denominator is positive in every constructed scenario. We aggregate with equal scenario weights,
\begin{equation}
 q_m(c)=\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}Q_{msc},\qquad
 \overline R_m(c)=\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}R_{msc},\qquad
 \overline A_m(c)=\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}A_{msc}.
 \label{eq:frontier}
\end{equation}
The primary outcome is $q_m(c)$. Exact accuracy treats a near-optimal bid as fully wrong, whereas the utility ratio records its economic cost. Equal weighting summarizes the declared scenario panel rather than a population distribution of bidder valuations. When a scenario--budget cell has repeated generations or option orders, we average replications within the scenario before averaging across scenarios. We treat reasoning tokens as a manipulable decision resource and refer to $\{q_m(c):c\in\mathcal C\}$ as the measured, discrete compute--quality frontier. We neither smooth this frontier nor impose monotonicity.

\subsection{Strategic reasoning-compute game}

In the reduced-form game, two modeled symmetric agents simultaneously select $c_i,c_j\in\mathcal C$. The empirically measured quality frontier assigned to these strategic contestants determines contest success through
\begin{equation}
 P_i(c_i,c_j;\gamma)=\frac{q(c_i)^\gamma}{q(c_i)^\gamma+q(c_j)^\gamma},\qquad
 \pi_i(c_i,c_j)=V P_i(c_i,c_j;\gamma)-\lambda c_i.
 \label{eq:contest}
\end{equation}
Here $V>0$ is the private prize, $\lambda$ is per-token cost, and $\gamma$ controls how strongly quality differences translate into winning. Dividing by $V$ yields
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde\pi_i=P_i(c_i,c_j;\gamma)-\underbrace{\frac{\lambda}{V}}_{\kappa}c_i,
 \qquad \boxed{\kappa=\lambda/V},
 \label{eq:normalizedprivate}
\end{equation}
so private choice depends on cost relative to the prize, not on their separate scales. The contest mapping is assumed rather than estimated.

A symmetric pure equilibrium $c_{\mathrm{NE}}$ is a mutual best response over the discrete menu. It represents decentralized budget choice within the modeled contest, conditional on the calibrated frontier. The evaluated language models do not choose compute or play this game.

The planner treats the prize as a transfer and values winner quality net of total compute:
\begin{equation}
 W_{\beta}(c_1,c_2)=\beta Q_\gamma(c_1,c_2)-\lambda(c_1+c_2),
 \quad Q_\gamma=P_1q(c_1)+P_2q(c_2).
\label{eq:welfare}
\end{equation}
After the same division,
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde W=\underbrace{\frac{\beta}{V}}_{\rho}Q_\gamma(c_1,c_2)
 -\kappa(c_1+c_2),\qquad \boxed{\rho=\beta/V}.
 \label{eq:normalizedwelfare}
\end{equation}
Thus every equilibrium and planner allocation is determined by $(\kappa,\rho,\gamma)$; $V$, $\lambda$, and $\beta$ are not independently meaningful scales in this game. Let $c_{\mathrm{NE}}$ be the lowest-budget symmetric mutual best response under exact ties, and let $(c_1^*,c_2^*)$ maximize Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalizedwelfare}. Define $C_{\mathrm{NE}}=2c_{\mathrm{NE}}$ and $C_{\mathrm{OPT}}=c_1^*+c_2^*$. We classify each parameter point as overcompute, aligned, or undercompute according as $C_{\mathrm{NE}}$ is greater than, equal to, or less than $C_{\mathrm{OPT}}$.

We retain $\mathrm{PoRC}=C_{\mathrm{NE}}/C_{\mathrm{OPT}}$ as a menu-dependent token diagnostic, not a welfare-loss measure. Normalized absolute welfare loss is $\Delta\widetilde W=\widetilde W_{\mathrm{OPT}}-\widetilde W_{\mathrm{NE}}=\Delta W/V$. The legacy relative-loss statistic and its fixed near-zero omission rule are retained only in the appendix; that cutoff is not scale invariant and does not define the phase regions.

The discrete thresholds make the comparative statics explicit. Against fixed $c_j$, a unilateral increase $c_a\to c_b$ is privately profitable when
\begin{equation}
 \kappa<\frac{P_i(c_b,c_j;\gamma)-P_i(c_a,c_j;\gamma)}{c_b-c_a}.
 \label{eq:privatethreshold}
\end{equation}
For two planner allocations differing by $(\Delta Q_\gamma,\Delta C)$, the higher-compute allocation is preferred when
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\kappa}{\rho}=\frac{\lambda}{\beta}<\frac{\Delta Q_\gamma}{\Delta C}.
 \label{eq:plannerthreshold}
\end{equation}
These numerical boundaries are properties of the stored frontier and the specified contest.

\section{Empirical methodology and experimental setup}

\subsection{Dataset and tasks}

We use the Online Auctions Dataset distributed with \citet{jank2010modeling}, containing 10,681 bid records from 628 eBay auctions \citep{onlineauctionsdataset}. The evaluation covers Palm Pilot M515 PDA, Xbox game console, and Cartier wristwatch auctions. Within each category, auctions with internally inconsistent opening bids are removed and the remaining auctions are deterministically assigned, using seed 20260819, to a 70\% construction split and a 30\% held-out scoring split. The construction split determines the displayed empirical win probabilities and task support; the held-out split is used only to score decisions. Historical closing prices are a reduced-form proxy for the price to beat, not observations of the highest rival valuation or a structural first-price-auction equilibrium.

For each category, 18 private values are selected from a five-dollar grid spanning the central-to-upper range of the construction-price distribution. At every value, we generate two independently seeded candidate sets for each option-set width $K\in\{5,7\}$, yielding $3\times18\times2\times2=216$ scenarios. Candidate bids are sampled from feasible five-dollar grid points using either a randomly located contiguous window or an unstructured sample. Candidate generation never conditions on the train- or test-optimal bid. Consequently, neither the identity nor the sorted position of the utility-maximizing option is fixed by construction.

The prompt provides the private value, candidate bids, and construction-sample win probabilities, but not expected utilities. The intended calculation is $(v-b)\widehat p_{\mathrm{construction}}(b)$. Primary regret, exact optimality, and utility ratio are instead evaluated with $\widehat p_{\mathrm{heldout}}(b)$, so performance rewards a decision that transfers to held-out auctions rather than one tailored to the displayed sample alone. Greedy evaluation repeats each scenario under option-order seeds $\{11,23,37\}$. The order is stored in every record and the candidate labels, rather than their displayed positions, determine scoring.

The unit of observation is a scenario--checkpoint--budget--replication cell. Holding the underlying scenario fixed across budgets creates a repeated-measures design, while repeated option orders and stochastic generations identify presentation and decoding variation separately. Calculator, uniform-random, median-position, highest-win-probability, and held-out-oracle policies provide explicit reference frontiers. The median policy is therefore a diagnostic for residual positional preference, not a rule that is correct by construction.

\subsection{Models and reasoning protocol}

We evaluate Phi-mini, Qwen3-4B, DeepSeek-8B, and Qwen3-8B \citep{abouelenin2025phi,yang2025qwen3,guo2025deepseek}; full repository IDs and environment details appear in Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}.

Every checkpoint uses the same constrained option-letter next-token readout, and a parallel free-form answer is retained for an agreement audit. The greedy reasoning frontier uses $\mathcal C_G=\{0,64,128,256,512,768\}$ under deterministic decoding and is repeated under option-order seeds $\{11,23,37\}$. A separate no-thinking condition uses budget zero. An unconstrained reasoning control may generate up to 1,024 tokens but is not treated as a fixed-budget action in the game.

Generation uncertainty is measured separately on $\mathcal C_S=\{128,384,768\}$ using stochastic seeds $\{1,7,21,42,99\}$, temperature 0.6, and top-$p$ 0.95. Option order is fixed at seed 314159 in this study so that between-seed variation reflects decoding rather than presentation. The runner records realized reasoning and answer tokens, elapsed time, NVML energy, average power, peak memory, answer log probability, constrained-answer probability, verbal confidence, and both constrained and free-form choices. GPU-rental and electricity costs are reported only when actual prices are supplied.

\subsection{Game computation}
\label{sec:gamecomputation}

For each model, we insert the stochastic frontier $q_m(c)$ on $\mathcal C_S$ directly into Eqs.~\eqref{eq:normalizedprivate}--\eqref{eq:normalizedwelfare}, without smoothing or interpolation. At each point we enumerate all ordered budget pairs, use the lowest-budget private best response under exact payoff ties, select the lowest symmetric fixed point if several remain, and separately maximize planner welfare. We use this frontier because five generations permit uncertainty propagation; no-thinking and unconstrained generations are not primary game actions. Appendix~\ref{app:zeroaugmented} separately augments $\mathcal C_S$ with the observed deterministic $c=0$ action as a deliberately protocol-mismatched sensitivity, not a fully stochastic four-budget frontier.

The phase analysis maps $\kappa\in[10^{-7},1.2\times10^{-3}]$ and $\rho\in[10^{-3},40]$ on 181 log-spaced points per axis for $\gamma\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}$. These bounds close the normalized values implied by the old parameter sweeps; neither the rectangle nor weights over it describe a market. The result is region location, not pixel share. We separately compute every exact payoff-indifference value in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:privatethreshold}--\eqref{eq:plannerthreshold} and verify allocations on either side.

Appendix~\ref{app:strategicrobustness} retains the old $V\in\{25,50,\ldots,1000\}$ by 60-point $\lambda\in[0.0001,0.03]$ summaries, eight $\beta$ values, and the same $\gamma$ sweep; each cell maps to $(\kappa,\rho)=(\lambda/V,\beta/V)$. It also retains the deterministic $\mathcal C_G$ game as a combined menu/frontier sensitivity, not a pure menu contrast because decoding changes. Appendix~\ref{app:computationalprotocol} gives tie rules.

\subsection{Uncertainty quantification}
\label{sec:uncertainty}

For bootstrap draw $b$, we sample scenario indices $I_1^{(b)},\ldots,I_S^{(b)}$ with replacement and apply the same index vector at every budget:
\begin{equation}
 q_m^{(b)}(c)=\frac{1}{S}\sum_{r=1}^{S}Q_{m,I_r^{(b)},c}.
 \label{eq:bootstrapfrontier}
\end{equation}
For the greedy frontier, the three option-order replications are averaged within each scenario--budget cell before scenarios are resampled. The paired resampling preserves cross-budget covariance because a selected scenario contributes its complete budget vector. We use 2,000 draws for endpoint differences and log-budget slopes. These intervals capture scenario-composition uncertainty after averaging over the declared order randomization.

For the stochastic frontier, a hierarchical bootstrap samples scenarios with replacement and then one generation seed for each selected scenario jointly across $\mathcal C_S$. The resulting frontier and game are recomputed in 1,000 draws. The zero-compute sensitivity uses the same sampled scenarios across all four hybrid actions, combines each selected scenario's order-averaged deterministic $q(0)$ with one jointly sampled stored stochastic seed at all positive budgets, and recomputes the exact game in 1,000 draws. Separately, 2,000 auction-level draws resample held-out auctions within product category and re-score the fixed stored choices against each empirical CDF. This last interval captures held-out CDF estimation only: it does not vary the construction CDF shown in prompts, split assignment, candidate sets, model choices, scenarios, or decoding. Varying any prompt-side CDF would require new model inference. Appendix~\ref{app:prospectiveuq} gives the full protocol; the final panel passed the coverage, duplicate-key, constrained-output-validity, and finite-value audit.

\subsection{Free-form readout sensitivity}
\label{sec:freeformmethod}

The constrained option letter remains the primary readout because it supplies a valid action for every model under one scoring rule. For sensitivity, we define a stored free-form response as valid only when its parsed bid exactly matches one of the displayed candidates, then recompute held-out utility from that candidate rather than reusing the constrained score. We report validity over all six deterministic budgets and constrained--free-form agreement conditional on validity. For the main endpoint check, we retain scenario--order cells with valid free-form bids at both 0 and 768 tokens, average paired differences over available orders within each scenario, and bootstrap scenarios 2,000 times. Per-budget available-case frontiers are descriptive because their composition changes with validity. We do not impute invalid responses, treat missingness as random, or propagate the incomplete free-form panel through the game.

\section{Results}
\FloatBarrier

\subsection{Leakage-free deterministic scaling}

Figure~\ref{fig:quality} and Table~\ref{tab:crossmodel} summarize the order-averaged greedy frontier over all 216 scenarios. Moving from 0 to 768 reasoning tokens raises held-out mean utility ratio by 0.175 for DeepSeek-8B, 0.111 for Phi-mini, 0.164 for Qwen3-4B, and 0.144 for Qwen3-8B. The paired scenario-bootstrap intervals are strictly positive for all four checkpoints (Table~\ref{tab:crossmodel}; two-sided bootstrap $p<0.001$ at the resolution of 2,000 draws). The corresponding 768-token utility ratios are 0.950, 0.829, 0.916, and 0.907, and exact-optimality rates are 64.7\%, 38.3\%, 57.4\%, and 55.2\%. All constrained outputs are valid.

The response is nevertheless not monotone at every adjacent budget. DeepSeek-8B falls between 0 and 64 tokens, Qwen3-4B falls at 128, and several intermediate increments are small. Most of the gain occurs at 512--768 tokens. A log-budget regression gives positive scenario-bootstrap slopes for all checkpoints: 0.0154 [0.0123, 0.0185] for DeepSeek-8B, 0.0117 [0.0081, 0.0151] for Phi-mini, 0.0185 [0.0144, 0.0228] for Qwen3-4B, and 0.0142 [0.0114, 0.0169] for Qwen3-8B. These regressions summarize association across the assigned budgets; they do not impose a structural scaling law.

\subsection{Baselines and task breadth}

The revised construction removes the earlier positional shortcut. The optimal option occupies every possible position in both the five- and seven-option tasks, and the median-position policy achieves only 0.725 mean utility ratio and 18.7\% accuracy. At 768 tokens, every checkpoint exceeds the median policy, uniform random choice (0.711), and the highest-win-probability rule (0.731). The construction-split calculator remains stronger than every fixed-budget model at 0.981, indicating that the prompt contains enough information to do better. Unconstrained generations score 0.972 for DeepSeek-8B, 0.846 for Phi-mini, 0.960 for Qwen3-4B, and 0.951 for Qwen3-8B. Because these controls are not budget-matched, they bound attainable performance rather than estimate a token effect.

\subsection{Free-form readout sensitivity}

Free-form validity is 29.1--74.3\% across checkpoints and constrained--free-form agreement conditional on validity is 32.7--71.3\% (Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:freeformsensitivity}--\ref{tab:freeformbybudget}). Among scenario--order cells valid at both endpoints, paired increases are 0.161--0.230 and all four bootstrap intervals exclude zero. Budget-dependent validity and only 47 paired Phi-mini scenarios make this an endpoint sensitivity, not a balanced replication or missingness-robust frontier.

\subsection{Generation uncertainty}

Five stochastic generations per scenario preserve the qualitative high-budget advantage. From 128 to 768 tokens, mean utility ratio increases from 0.758 to 0.947 for DeepSeek-8B, 0.746 to 0.813 for Phi-mini, 0.730 to 0.914 for Qwen3-4B, and 0.775 to 0.893 for Qwen3-8B. The middle budget can still be flat or worse---Phi-mini changes from 0.746 at 128 to 0.744 at 384---so repeated sampling does not make the frontier mechanically monotone. Hierarchical intervals for the complete stochastic frontier appear in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:stochasticfrontier}.

The separate held-out auction-CDF bootstrap also preserves the deterministic endpoint direction: re-scored increases are 0.171 [0.152, 0.187], 0.111 [0.092, 0.130], 0.160 [0.146, 0.173], and 0.140 [0.128, 0.150] in the same model order (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:auctioncdf}). These intervals condition on the original prompts and choices and must not be conflated with the scenario or generation bootstraps.

\subsection{Calibrated strategic reasoning-compute results}
\label{sec:strategicresults}

Figure~\ref{fig:normalizedphase} shows the strategic phase diagram. Every model has overcompute, aligned, and undercompute regions at $\gamma=1$. At low $\kappa$, private compute is high. As $\kappa$ rises, equilibrium compute for each modeled agent falls from 768 to 128 tokens at $3.34\times10^{-5}$ for Phi-mini, $5.49\times10^{-5}$ for Qwen3-8B, and $8.65\times10^{-5}$ for DeepSeek-8B. Qwen3-4B instead moves $768\to384\to128$ at $8.39\times10^{-5}$ and $9.28\times10^{-5}$. These vertical transitions do not depend on $\rho$.

Planner compute depends on $r=\kappa/\rho=\lambda/\beta$. As $r$ rises, its total allocation moves $1536\to896\to256$ tokens; across models the first threshold ranges from $4.99\times10^{-5}$ to $1.31\times10^{-4}$ and the second from $5.44\times10^{-5}$ to $1.64\times10^{-4}$. The resulting diagonal boundaries create an overcompute wedge where private incentives still support high compute but social quality value is low, and an undercompute region where high $\rho$ supports planner compute after private demand has fallen. Gray regions have equal total compute, although the underlying allocation can differ. These boundaries come from the measured point frontiers and exact enumeration, not from phase-grid frequencies.

Increasing $\gamma$ strengthens private incentives for compute. Across the four frontiers, private transition thresholds move from $(1.67$--$4.64)\times10^{-5}$ at $\gamma=0.5$ to $(1.33$--$3.67)\times10^{-4}$ at $\gamma=4$, expanding the overcompute region (Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:normalizedphasegamma}). Increasing $\rho$ instead lowers $\kappa/\rho$, raises planner compute, and moves a point upward from overcomputation through alignment toward undercomputation. The sensitivity reveals the economic comparative statics of the calibrated game.

Allowing a true zero-reasoning action through the stored deterministic $c=0$ measurements changes several exact thresholds and makes zero optimal at sufficiently high relative cost, but preserves overcompute, aligned, and undercompute regions for every checkpoint and every tested $\gamma$; all three regions also appear in every one of 1,000 hybrid bootstrap draws (Appendix~\ref{app:zeroaugmented}). Because $q(0)$ and the positive-budget observations use different presentation and decoding protocols, this is supporting sensitivity evidence rather than a replacement for the primary stochastic game.

The old raw-grid summaries provide a secondary description. At $\beta=\gamma=1$, excess-compute shares are 56.2--83.4\% across checkpoints; increasing $\beta$ to 1000 makes all those old cells non-excess, while increasing $\gamma$ from 0.5 to 4 raises the old-grid shares (Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:strategicsensitivity}--\ref{tab:menurobustness}). These percentages and their bootstrap intervals apply only to the specified grid. PoRC remains a menu-dependent diagnostic, and the legacy welfare-loss scale is not identified. On the deterministic six-budget frontier, the old-grid excess shares remain 72.3--79.5\% (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:menurobustness}), although this comparison changes the decoding regime as well as the menu.

\section{Discussion}

Across the four checkpoints, the endpoint gains persist across candidate sets, option orders, products, and stochastic generations. They also survive the valid-response free-form analysis, although budget-dependent missingness limits comparisons under that readout. The intermediate budgets are less regular: several steps are flat or negative, and the calculator remains stronger than every fixed-budget model. Reasoning compute improves the models' auction calculations on average, but an exact algorithm still has a clear advantage on this structured task.

The contest model asks how these measured returns interact with competitive incentives. Its normalization makes the sources of misallocation transparent. Relative compute cost $\kappa$ governs private demand, $\kappa/\rho$ governs the planner's demand, and $\gamma$ determines how strongly a quality advantage affects contest success. The phase diagram therefore admits both overcomputation and undercomputation. The $\beta$ and $\gamma$ sweeps illustrate the same logic: raising $\beta$ at fixed $(V,\lambda)$ increases $\rho$ and planner compute without changing $\kappa$, while raising $\gamma$ moves private indifference thresholds toward higher relative cost. Sensitivity to these parameters is economically informative because the data do not identify a distribution over $(\kappa,\rho,\gamma)$. For the same reason, the original grid shares and PoRC should be read as diagnostics of the declared parameterization.

A market interpretation would require additional evidence. Monetary runtime and prize calibration would locate $\kappa$; an empirical value for decision quality would locate $\rho$; and competitive outcomes would identify the contest-success mapping and $\gamma$. Direct interaction would test whether deployed AI systems adjust budgets in response to rivals. Until these quantities are measured, the phase diagram organizes hypotheses about compute competition rather than supporting policy prescriptions.

\section{Limitations}

Candidate construction is independent of the optimum and option order is randomized, so the median position no longer reveals the correct action. Even with these controls, the auction task remains constructed. Closing prices are a reduced-form historical proxy, private values come from a declared grid, and the model selects from a finite candidate set rather than bidding against endogenous rivals. The construction/held-out split measures transfer across auctions within the same product category; it does not establish temporal or cross-market generalization.

The strategic analysis begins only after $q(c)$ has been estimated. The LLMs neither choose budgets nor play against one another, so the Nash--planner gap is a property of the calibrated model rather than demonstrated LLM behavior. Direct-game outputs are not used, and the contest-success function is assumed rather than learned from head-to-head outcomes. Normalization removes redundant nominal scales but does not identify $\kappa$, $\rho$, $\gamma$, or their distribution in any market. The game further assumes symmetric modeled agents, a fixed prize, and linear cost. Its conclusions may change with asymmetric agents, mixed equilibria, nonlinear costs, or alternative success functions. The compute-menu checks also have limits: the six-budget sensitivity changes the decoding regime, while the zero-compute analysis combines order-averaged deterministic $q(0)$ with fixed-order stochastic positive-budget observations. Neither isolates menu granularity under the primary stochastic protocol.

External validity is limited by the four checkpoints, 216 scenarios, and three products. The scenario, generation, and held-out-auction bootstraps omit market shift, new prompt-side CDFs, split assignments, model misspecification, and contest-mapping uncertainty. Evaluating a new construction split would require additional inference, and free-form missingness prevents a complete alternative game. Tokens are also an imperfect proxy for compute, and the absence of monetary calibration prevents us from locating a deployed system in the phase diagram. Exact model revisions were not logged contemporaneously; Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} reports the snapshots recovered from the preserved cache and the associated attribution caveat. Finally, the welfare calculation omits environmental and latency externalities, redistribution, heterogeneous valuations, and intrinsic prize value.

\section{Conclusion}

Across four checkpoints, increasing deterministic reasoning from 0 to 768 tokens improves held-out economic utility by 0.111--0.175. The gains are robust at the endpoints, although they are not monotone at every intermediate budget. We use the resulting compute--utility frontiers as inputs to a normalized contest model, which produces overcompute, aligned, and undercompute regions as $\kappa$, $\rho$, and $\gamma$ vary. Thus the economic value of reasoning can support different forms of compute misallocation under different market primitives. Establishing which region describes a deployed setting will require monetary calibration, observed strategic compute choices, and an empirically identified contest technology.

\section{Computational and runner protocols}
\label{app:computationalprotocol}

\subsection{Equilibrium enumeration and tie handling}
For every ordered pair $(c_i,c_j)$ in $\mathcal C_S$, we calculate contest success and both private payoffs. For each opposing action, the implementation selects the lowest-budget best response under exact payoff ties; if more than one symmetric fixed point remains, it reports the lowest. Planner welfare is evaluated for every ordered allocation and the first lexicographic maximizer is reported under an exact tie. Swapping the two modeled agents in a planner optimum leaves total compute unchanged. At a transition where planner optima with different totals tie, the boundary itself is an indifference locus; the threshold table reports allocations in the adjacent open regions. All point and bootstrap results use these same deterministic rules. No direct-play outcome enters the paper.

\subsection{Grid estimands}
The primary strategic output is the classification map in normalized $(\kappa,\rho,\gamma)$ space and its exact discrete transition boundaries. For each point we retain total Nash and planner compute and normalized welfare loss $\Delta\widetilde W$. The old unweighted excess-compute share, conditional PoRC, and unscaled welfare summaries are retained as finite-grid diagnostics: changing raw-grid bounds, spacing, or weights changes every aggregate. They are not estimates under an empirical market distribution.

\subsection{Scenario-bootstrap scope}
In the greedy paired bootstrap, a sampled scenario contributes its order-averaged complete budget vector, preserving within-scenario covariance. Each draw recomputes endpoint changes and log-budget slopes without a normal approximation or monotonicity restriction. The resulting intervals target finite-scenario composition conditional on the prompts and order-replication design. The hierarchical stochastic bootstrap additionally samples generation seeds and propagates each sampled frontier through every game cell. Neither procedure covers market primitives, model misspecification, contest-function misspecification, or distribution shift.

\subsection{Generation and confidence protocol}
\label{app:prospectiveuq}
The runner evaluates all four checkpoints on independently generated five- and seven-option candidate sets from Palm Pilot, Xbox, and Cartier auctions. Auctions are divided deterministically 70/30 into construction and held-out scoring samples. Candidate sets are generated without conditioning on the optimal bid, option order is randomized across three greedy replications, and a common constrained option-letter readout is used for every checkpoint. The deterministic frontier uses $\mathcal C_G=\{0,64,128,256,512,768\}$; an unconstrained generation with a 1,024-token cap is a diagnostic control, not a game action.

Generation uncertainty is evaluated separately on $\mathcal C_S=\{128,384,768\}$ with seeds $\{1,7,21,42,99\}$, temperature $0.6$, and nucleus threshold $0.95$. Option order is held fixed at seed 314159 so variation across seeds reflects decoding rather than presentation order. Scenario-clustered intervals summarize utility ratio, regret, exact optimality, and bid dispersion. In each of 1,000 hierarchical-bootstrap draws, scenarios are sampled with replacement and one generation seed is sampled for each selected scenario, using that seed jointly at all three budgets. The resulting frontier is propagated through the same 2,400-cell economic grid and reported as a distinct three-budget stochastic game, separate from the six-budget greedy game.

The runner records the geometric mean probability assigned to the constrained answer and a separate verbal-confidence report. A deterministic, scenario-disjoint 40/60 calibration/evaluation split is used for Brier score, negative log likelihood, ten-bin expected calibration error, AUROC, and risk--coverage analysis, followed by calibrated stopping or abstention policies. These analyses identify generation and reported-confidence uncertainty under the stated protocol only; they do not identify model misspecification, market shift, or contest-mapping uncertainty.

\subsection{Budget enforcement, readout, and exact prompt}
\label{app:reproducibility}

\paragraph{Code and reproducibility.}
Code, prompt templates, saved outputs, analysis scripts, and reproduction
instructions are available in the anonymous repository at
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/reasoning-compute-economic-utility-EED7/}.

The same enforcement code is used for Phi, Qwen, and DeepSeek. The Hugging Face chat template is called with \texttt{enable\_thinking=True} in reasoning mode when the tokenizer accepts that argument. For a positive assigned budget $c$, generation sets both \texttt{min\_new\_tokens} and \texttt{max\_new\_tokens} to $c$ and suppresses EOS token IDs. Thus exactly $c$ token IDs are recorded as reasoning, even if the trace ends mid-sentence or would otherwise stop earlier; the cap is a token boundary, not a semantic end-of-thought boundary. Greedy traces use \texttt{do\_sample=False}; stochastic traces use temperature 0.6 and top-$p$ 0.95. The prompt template is:

\begin{quote}\footnotesize\ttfamily
You are a bidder in a first-price auction.\\
Your private value is \$\{value\}.\\
Available bids are shown in randomized order:\\
Option A: bid \$\{bid\}; estimated win probability \{probability\}\\
One line is supplied per displayed option.\\
For any bid b, expected profit under the supplied estimate is\\
(private value - b) * estimated win probability.\\
Choose one listed bid. Do not infer the answer from option position.\\
End exactly with: FINAL BID: <number>
\end{quote}

After the reasoning trace, the runner appends \texttt{Now give the required answer. FINAL BID:} and greedily allows at most 20 answer tokens; this answer can stop at EOS and is retained as the free-form readout. The primary action is then obtained in a separate deterministic forward pass over the original prompt, saved reasoning trace, and the suffix \texttt{Use the reasoning above and select the best displayed option. Return one option letter only. FINAL OPTION:}. The runner takes the argmax only among the single-token labels A through E or G, as applicable, and maps that label to the displayed bid. Final-answer and constrained-readout tokens are not counted in $c$.

At $c=0$, the reasoning generation call is skipped and the empty trace is passed directly to the two answer stages. Operationally, ``0 reasoning tokens'' therefore means no generated scratchpad, not an attempt to truncate a native trace after generation. The separate no-thinking control also requests \texttt{enable\_thinking=False} when supported; if a tokenizer rejects the flag, the runner falls back to its default chat template, but still generates no scratchpad at budget zero. The unconstrained control is different: it permits up to 1,024 generated tokens and is not an action in either reported game.

\subsection{Confidence and adaptive-sampling diagnostic}

Answer-token probability contains ranking information but is not calibrated as a correctness probability. At 768 tokens its held-out AUROC ranges from 0.699 to 0.760 and ten-bin ECE from 0.242 to 0.391. Verbal-confidence AUROC ranges from 0.391 to 0.500. A confidence-weighted convergence policy uses 3.18--3.67 samples on average and approximately matches five-sample utility for DeepSeek-8B, Phi-mini, and Qwen3-4B, but trails it for Qwen3-8B (0.923 versus 0.929). This non-central diagnostic supports model-specific calibration rather than a universal confidence threshold.

\subsection{Readout, integrity, and completion audit}
The constrained option letter defines the primary action because it is available under the same scoring rule for every checkpoint. The original free-form response is retained and independently parsed; disagreement is never overwritten. Across the six deterministic budgets, free-form validity is 66.8\% for DeepSeek-8B, 29.1\% for Phi-mini, 74.3\% for Qwen3-4B, and 51.0\% for Qwen3-8B. Agreement conditional on validity is 46.6\%, 32.7\%, 54.5\%, and 71.3\%, respectively. Section~\ref{sec:freeformmethod} defines the paired endpoint sensitivity. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:freeformbybudget} exposes the strong budget dependence of validity; no invalid response is imputed or scored.

Each checkpoint contains the complete balanced design: 648 no-thinking rows, 4,536 greedy-reasoning rows, and 3,240 stochastic-reasoning rows, with no duplicate keys. The audit verifies all 216 scenarios, three product categories, both candidate-set widths, three greedy order seeds, and five generation seeds at every stochastic budget. The exported validation report passes with no errors; its only substantive warnings are the intentionally absent direct-game files and unavailable dollar-cost calibration.

\section{Broader impacts}
The measurement framework may help developers allocate inference resources using decision utility rather than exact-answer accuracy and may expose settings in which duplicated compute deserves further empirical study. Conversely, treating the conditional game grid as evidence about deployed markets could motivate unjustified compute restrictions or policy claims. Larger reasoning budgets also increase energy and latency even when their economic benefit is small. We mitigate these risks by reporting analytic baselines, normalization and menu sensitivities, explicit missing calibrations, and by avoiding recommendations about taxes, caps, or deployment policy.
